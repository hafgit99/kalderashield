Product / Passkeys

Passkeys that never leave your vault

Create and sign WebAuthn passkeys inside your encrypted local vault. Approve logins from the desktop modal, browser extension, or Android 14+ Credential Manager — with zero cloud dependency.

What it does

Passwordless sign-in, owned by your vault

KalderaShield acts as a standards-compliant WebAuthn Passkey Provider. When a site asks to create or use a passkey, the browser extension or Android 14+ Credential Manager forwards the request to your local vault, where an ECDSA P-256 key pair is generated and signed after your explicit approval.

Every passkey private key is wrapped inside the vault with AES-256-GCM and HKDF-SHA256 using your active vault key. Private keys never leave KalderaShield in plaintext and are never sent to the browser or any third-party cloud.

If you prefer a hardware token for a specific account, every approval prompt includes a one-click fallback to Windows Hello, Touch ID, Android biometrics, or a FIDO2 security key such as YubiKey.

How a passkey sign-in works

A site requests a passkey

The browser extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) or Android 14+ Credential Manager intercepts the WebAuthn call and validates the site's origin and relying party ID.

You approve inside KalderaShield

KalderaShield brings forward a trusted approval dialog showing the exact domain and account. Nothing is signed until the vault is unlocked and you confirm.

The vault signs and returns only the assertion

KalderaShield signs the WebAuthn challenge with the vault-encrypted ES256 private key, increments the signature counter, and returns only the public assertion to the site.

Security Architecture

What passkeys do and do not replace

Portable with your encrypted backups

Because vault-backed passkeys are stored encrypted inside your KalderaShield vault, they travel safely with your encrypted backups and survive losing a device — without relying on a vendor cloud. Hardware-bound passkeys created on a YubiKey or platform authenticator stay pinned to that device. In both cases, passkeys replace site passwords rather than your vault's master password and secret key.

WebAuthn / ES256

Standards-compliant, not a private format

Credentials produce standard CBOR/COSE ECDSA P-256 attestations and signed assertions that work with any compliant WebAuthn relying party.

AEAD Köprüsü

Private keys never touch the browser

Desktop browser extension requests cross an authenticated AEAD native messaging channel, and Android 14+ uses a non-exported Credential Manager relay with 128-bit tokens.

AES-256-GCM

Encrypted like every other vault item

Each passkey private key is wrapped with HKDF-SHA256 + AES-256-GCM, re-wrapped automatically on master password rotation, and backed up with your vault.

Frequently Asked Questions

About passkeys

Do I still need my master password?

Yes. Your vault is still unlocked by your master password and secret key (or your configured biometric/FIDO2 unlock). A passkey replaces the password on the sites where you register one.

Where can I use KalderaShield passkeys?

On desktop via the KalderaShield browser extension (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) paired with the desktop app, and on Android 14+ via Android Credential Manager for mobile browsers and apps. You can also fall back to Windows Hello, Touch ID, or YubiKey on any prompt.

What if I lose my device?

Passkeys created inside your KalderaShield vault are encrypted inside your vault backups, so restoring your encrypted backup on a new device restores your passkeys too. Any passkey you chose to store only on a hardware authenticator remains tied to that device.
